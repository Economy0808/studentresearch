# DRAFT — additive combinatorics pack (high context, three lineages)

## Definitions

Throughout, $\mathbb N=\{1,2,3,\dots\}$, $[N]=\{1,\dots,N\}$, $p$ denotes a prime and $\mathbb F_p=\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z$. For positive functions, $f\ll g$ (equivalently $f=O(g)$) means $f\le Cg$ for a constant $C$; a subscript, as in $\ll_\varepsilon$, means $C$ may depend on that parameter; $f=o(g)$ means $f/g\to0$; $f=\Omega(g)$ means $g\ll f$.

**Sumsets.** For subsets $A,B$ of an abelian group, $A+B=\{a+b:a\in A,\ b\in B\}$, $A-B=\{a-b:a\in A,\ b\in B\}$, $hA=A+\dots+A$ ($h$ summands) and $kA-lA$ similarly; $\lambda\cdot A=\{\lambda a:a\in A\}$ is a dilate.

**Arithmetic progressions.** A $k$-term arithmetic progression ($k$-AP) is $x,x+d,\dots,x+(k-1)d$; it is non-trivial if $d\ne0$. $r_k(N)$ is the largest size of a subset of $[N]$ with no non-trivial $k$-AP.

**Densities.** The density of $A\subseteq[N]$ is $|A|/N$; for $A\subseteq G$ finite, $|A|/|G|$.

The three sections below each follow one line of research from its origin to the state of knowledge in November 2026. Definitions specific to each line follow.

**For “Sets without three-term progressions”.**

- **3-AP-free in an abelian group.** In an abelian group $G$, a 3-term progression is a triple of distinct $x,y,z$ with $x+z=2y$. $r_3(G)$ is the largest size of a subset of $G$ with none; $r_3(N)$ is the integer case $G=[N]$ (no nontrivial progression), and $r_3(\mathbb F_p^n)$ is the case of a fixed odd prime $p$ and $n\to\infty$.
- **Cap set.** A subset of $\mathbb F_3^n$ with no solution of $x+y+z=0$ other than $x=y=z$. Since $x+z=2y\iff x+y+z=0$ in $\mathbb F_3^n$, cap sets are exactly the 3-AP-free subsets of $\mathbb F_3^n$.
- **Bohr set, density increment.** A Bohr set of rank $d$ and radius $\rho$ in $\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z$ is the set of $x$ for which $d$ given frequencies $\gamma_ix/N$ are all within $\rho$ of an integer; it plays the role of a subspace. A density increment argument shows that a progression-free set of density $\alpha$ has larger relative density on a translate of a subspace (or Bohr set) and iterates until the density would exceed 1.
- **Sifting and almost-periodicity.** Two steps of the Kelley–Meka scheme. With $\mu_A=\frac{|G|}{|A|}1_A$ and $\mu_A\circ\mu_A(x)=\mathbb E_y\mu_A(y)\mu_A(x+y)$, sifting finds sparse sets $A_1,A_2$ whose difference-convolution lives on the large values of $\mu_A\circ\mu_A$; the Croot–Sisask almost-periodicity lemma then lets one average over a structured set to reach a subspace or Bohr set.
- **Slice rank, polynomial method.** A tensor $f(x,y,z)$ has slice rank at most $r$ if it is a sum of $r$ terms of the form $g(x)h(y,z)$, $g(y)h(x,z)$ or $g(z)h(x,y)$. The diagonal tensor on $A\times A\times A$ has slice rank $|A|$; bounding the slice rank of $\prod_i(1-(x_i+y_i+z_i)^2)$ by counting monomials bounds $|A|$ (Croot–Lev–Pach, Ellenberg–Gijswijt, Tao).
- **Direct product, extended product.** If $A\subseteq\mathbb F_3^n$ and $B\subseteq\mathbb F_3^m$ are cap sets then $A\times B\subseteq\mathbb F_3^{n+m}$ is one. An extended product (Edel, Tyrrell) takes a union of products of blocks, each block a (transformed) copy of a cap set or $\{0\}$, arranged by an "admissible set" of block patterns.
- **Genus-one translation-invariant equation.** A linear equation $c_1x_1+\dots+c_sx_s=0$ over $\mathbb F_q$ with all $c_i\ne0$, $\sum c_i=0$, and $\sum_{i\in I}c_i\ne0$ for every nonempty proper $I$. A set is $L$-free if its only solutions have $x_1=\dots=x_s$.
- **Behrend-type shell.** Take vectors whose coordinates lie in a small box and which have one fixed Euclidean norm; strict convexity of the sphere forbids progressions, and a base-$d$ digit map turns this into a subset of $\mathbb Z$ or of $\mathbb F_p^n$ (using only coordinates $0,\dots,(p-1)/2$ to avoid wrap-around).

**For “Sets with small doubling”.**

- **Doubling.** $A$ has doubling at most $K$ if $|A+A|\le K|A|$; write $\sigma[A]=|A+A|/|A|$ and $\sigma_-[A]=|A-A|/|A|$.
- **Torsion $m$.** An abelian group $G$ has torsion $m$ if $mg=0$ for all $g\in G$ (e.g. $\mathbb F_p^n$ with $m=p$).
- **Proper $d$-dimensional progression.** $P=\{a_0+l_1a_1+\dots+l_da_d:1\le l_i\le L_i\}$ with $|P|=L_1\cdots L_d$.
- **Convex progression, convex coset progression.** A $d$-dimensional convex progression is $\phi(\mathbb Z^d\cap Q)$ for a centrally symmetric convex body $Q\subset\mathbb R^d$ and a homomorphism $\phi:\mathbb Z^d\to G$. A convex coset progression is $H+P$ with $H\le G$ a subgroup and $P$ a convex progression.
- **Entropic Ruzsa distance.** For $G$-valued random variables, $d[X;Y]=\mathbb H(\tilde X-\tilde Y)-\tfrac12\mathbb H(\tilde X)-\tfrac12\mathbb H(\tilde Y)$ with $\tilde X,\tilde Y$ independent copies and $\mathbb H$ Shannon entropy. $U_H$ is the uniform law on a subgroup $H$.
- **Freiman $s$-isomorphism.** A bijection between sets preserving all additive relations $x_1+\dots+x_s=y_1+\dots+y_s$ in both directions.
- **Polynomial Bogolyubov statement.** For $|A+A|\le K|A|$ (torsion $m$), there is a subgroup $H\subseteq 2A-2A$ with $|H|\ge K^{-O_m(1)}|A|$.

**For “Restricted sumsets”.**

- **Restricted sumset.** For finite $A,B$ in an abelian group and a set $R\subseteq A\times B$ of *forbidden pairs*, $A+_R B=\{a+b : a\in A,\ b\in B,\ (a,b)\notin R\}$. Taking $R=\{(a,a)\}$ gives $A\dot+B$, the sums of two *distinct* elements; for a polynomial $P(x,y)$ one writes $A+^{P}B=\{a+b : P(a,b)\neq 0\}$. Throughout, $p$ is prime and $\mathbb F_p=\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z$.
- **Degree, matching, function relation.** Regard $R$ as a bipartite graph on $A\sqcup B$; its *degree* is the largest number of pairs of $R$ through one element ("degree on $B$" counts only elements of $B$). $R$ is a *matching* if the degree is $\le 1$ on both sides; "$R$ induced by a map $\tau$" means $R=\{(a,\tau(a))\}$ (or $\{(f(b),b)\}$), and an injective map induces a matching.
- **Cauchy–Davenport.** For nonempty $A,B\subseteq\mathbb F_p$, $|A+B|\ge\min\{p,|A|+|B|-1\}$.
- **Rectifiable pair.** $(A,B)$ with $A,B\subseteq\mathbb F_p$ is rectifiable if there are injections $f,g$ into $\mathbb Z$ with $a+b=a'+b' \iff f(a)+g(b)=f(a')+g(b')$, i.e. the pair behaves additively like a pair of integer sets.
- **Sum of dilates; constant $H$.** For $\lambda_i\in\mathbb C$, $A+\lambda_1\!\cdot\! A+\dots+\lambda_k\!\cdot\! A=\{a_0+\lambda_1a_1+\dots+\lambda_ka_k\}$. For algebraic $\lambda_i$ put $K=\mathbb Q(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_k)$, $d=[K:\mathbb Q]$, complex embeddings $\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_d$, denominator ideal $\mathfrak D=\{x\in\mathcal O_K : x\lambda_l\in\mathcal O_K\ \forall l\}$, and $H(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_k)=[\mathcal O_K:\mathfrak D]\prod_{i=1}^d\bigl(1+|\sigma_i(\lambda_1)|+\dots+|\sigma_i(\lambda_k)|\bigr)$. For a single $\lambda$ with minimal polynomial $\prod_i(a_ix+b_i)\in\mathbb Z[x]$ (coprime coefficients), $H(\lambda)=\prod_i(|a_i|+|b_i|)$; e.g. $H(\sqrt2)=(1+\sqrt2)^2$.
- **Irreducible, coprime, pre-commuting matrices.** $L_0,\dots,L_k\in\mathrm{Mat}_d(\mathbb Z)$ are *irreducible* if no nontrivial subspaces $U,V\subseteq\mathbb Q^d$ of equal dimension satisfy $L_iU\subseteq V$ for all $i$; *coprime* if no $P,Q\in GL_d(\mathbb Q)$ with $0<|\det P\det Q|<1$ make every $PL_iQ$ integral; *pre-commuting* if some $P\in GL_d(\mathbb Q)$ makes $PL_0,\dots,PL_k$ pairwise commute.
- **Iterated logarithm.** $\log_{(k)}$ is the $k$-fold iterated logarithm.

## Result: Sets without three-term arithmetic progressions

**Origin.** Erdős and Turán posed in 1936 the problem of the largest $A\subseteq[N]$ with no nontrivial three-term progression (2406.12290, 2603.27045). The analogous problem in $\mathbb F_p^n$ (the finite field model), especially the cap set problem in $\mathbb F_3^n$, was popularized in the hope that better bounds there would transfer to $[N]$ (2406.12290).

**Milestones.**
- *Lower bounds in $\mathbb Z$.* Salem–Spencer (1942) and Behrend (1946, PNAS 32) gave $r_3(N)=N^{1-o(1)}$; Behrend: $r_3(N)\ge N\,2^{-(2\sqrt2+o(1))\sqrt{\log_2N}}$, by a sphere-shell construction. Elkin (2010, Israel J. Math. 184) improved the lower-order factor to a gain of $(\log_2N)^{1/4}$; Green–Wolf (2010) gave another proof. All of this changed only the $o(1)$ (2406.12290).
- *Upper bounds in $\mathbb Z$, density increment era.* Roth (1953): $r_3(N)=o(N)$, quantitatively $O(N/\log\log N)$. Heath-Brown (1987) and Szemerédi (1990): $N(\log N)^{-\delta}$ for some $0<\delta<1/2$. Bourgain (1999), via Bohr sets: $\ll N\sqrt{\log\log N/\log N}$. Bourgain (2008) and Sanders (2011, 2012): $N(\log N)^{-1+o(1)}$. Bloom–Sisask (2020, arXiv:2007.03528): $N(\log N)^{-1-c}$ (2408.02328; 2603.27045).
- *Kelley–Meka (FOCS 2023).* $r_3(N)\le N\exp(-c(\log N)^{1/12})$, matching the quasipolynomial shape of Behrend's bound; the survey 2408.02328 quotes exponent $1/11$. Bloom–Sisask (2023, arXiv:2309.02353) improved the exponent to $1/9$ by a better bootstrapping step. Kelley–Meka also give, over $\mathbb F_p^n$, $\ll 2^{-\kappa_pn^{1/9}}p^n$.
- *Finite-field upper bounds.* Brown–Buhler (1982) and Frankl–Graham–Rödl (1987): $r_3(G)=o(|G|)$. Meshulam (1995): $r_3(\mathbb F_p^n)<c_pp^n/n$ (density increment on codimension-one subspaces; Lev 2012 gave a combinatorial proof). Lev (2004): $r_3(G)<2|G|/\mathrm{rank}(2G)$. Sanders (2009): $o(4^n/n)$ in $\mathbb Z_4^n$. Bateman–Katz (2012): $r_3(\mathbb F_3^n)\ll3^n/n^{1+\varepsilon}$.
- *Polynomial method.* Croot–Lev–Pach (Ann. Math. 185, 2017): $r_3(\mathbb Z_4^n)\le3.611^n$. Ellenberg–Gijswijt (Ann. Math. 185, 2017): for every prime $p\ge3$, $r_3(\mathbb F_p^n)\le(c_pp)^n$ with $c_p<1$, $c_p\to0.841\ldots$ as $p\to\infty$, and $r_3(\mathbb F_3^n)\le(2.7552\ldots)^n$. Tao's symmetric formulation reduces it to the slice rank of a diagonal tensor.
- *Cap set lower bounds.* Edel (2004): $2.217389^n$; Tyrrell (2023, Discrete Anal. 2023:20): $2.218^n$; later records quoted in the literature: $2.2202^n$ (Romera-Paredes et al., Nature 2024; quoted in 2406.12290) and $2.2203\ldots^n$ (current record quoted in 2606.12194). In $\mathbb F_5^n$ the best known bound is $35^{1/3}\approx3.271$ per coordinate (Elsholtz–Lipnik–Siebenhofer, Pollak; 2406.12290). Earlier general lower bound: $((p+1)/2)^{n-o(n)}$ in $\mathbb F_p^n$ (Salem–Spencer/Behrend adaptation; refined to $o(n)$ error by Elsholtz–Pach 2020).
- *Elsholtz–Hunter–Proske–Sauermann (arXiv:2406.12290, 2024).* Theorem 1.1: $r_3(N)\ge N2^{-(C+o(1))\sqrt{\log_2N}}$ with $C=2\sqrt{\log_2(24/7)}\approx2.667<2\sqrt2$; the proof gives $N2^{-C\sqrt{\log_2N}}/(10^9(\log_2N)^{3/2})$. Theorem 1.2: some $c>1/2$ with $r_3(\mathbb F_p^n)\ge(cp)^n$ for every prime $p$ and $n$ large; any $c<\sqrt{7/24}\approx0.540$ works. Theorem 1.3: $r_3(\mathbb Z_{m_1}\times\dots\times\mathbb Z_{m_n})\ge(7/24)^{n/2}m_1\cdots m_n/(10^6m^2n^3)$ for $2\le m_i\le m$. Idea: embed randomly in the torus $[0,1)^n$; take a slice of $T^{n/2}$ for a 2-dimensional polygonal region $T\subseteq[0,1)^2$ of area $\ge7/24-\varepsilon$ with $f:T\to[0,100/\varepsilon^2]$ satisfying $f(x)+f(z)\ge2f(y)+|x-z|^2$ whenever $x+z\equiv2y\pmod 1$; this forces the ends of any torus progression within the slice to be close, which the lattice embedding rules out for distinct points. Behrend's construction corresponds to area $1/4$.
- *Raghavan (arXiv:2603.27045, preprint, 2026).* Same scheme as Kelley–Meka and Bloom–Sisask: (1) Hölder lifting and unbalancing, $\|\mu_A\circ\mu_A\|_p$ large for $p=O(\log(1/\alpha))$; (2) sifting; (3) almost-periodicity; (4) bootstrapping to a subspace or Bohr set $V$; (5) density increment. New: sifting is iterated $O(\log(1/\alpha))$ times (a weighted variant of sifting handles Bohr sets), and Bohr-set radii are tracked so closely that the final Bohr set has size $\exp(-O(\log^6(1/\alpha)\log\log(1/\alpha)))$ relative to $[N]$, which produces $N\le\exp(O(L^6\log L))$ with $L=\log(2/\alpha)$. Over $\mathbb F_q^n$ the same argument gives $\exp(-\Omega(\log^{1/5}N))N$ (Theorem 1.5).\n- *Hametner–Tyrrell (arXiv:2606.12194, 2026).* For a cap set $A\subseteq\mathbb F_3^n$ and every $r\ge1$ there is a cap set $B\subseteq\mathbb F_3^{2nr}$ with $|B|=r|A|^{2r-2}$; with $r>|A|^2$ this beats $|A|^{1/n}$. Proof idea: translate so $0\notin A$; put $A_1=A\times A$ and $A_2=\{(x+y,x-y):x,y\in A\}$, cap sets in $\mathbb F_3^{2n}$ with $0\notin A_1\cup A_2$ and $0\notin A_1+A_2$; take the union over $j\le r$ of $A_1^{j-1}\times\{0\}\times A_2^{r-j}$, which has no nontrivial solutions by a three-case check. So no fixed cap set gives an asymptotically optimal lower bound by direct products. Genus-one equations: with $d=(2^s-s-2)(1+2s(s-1))$, an $L$-free $A\subseteq\mathbb F_q^n$ yields $L$-free $B\subseteq\mathbb F_q^{dnr}$ with $|B|=r|A|^{d(r-1)}$.

**Current frontier (November 2026).**
- *$[N]$, upper:* $|A|\le N\exp(-(\log N)^{1/6-o(1)})$, more precisely $\exp(-\Omega(\log^{1/6}N\cdot(\log\log N)^{-1/6}))N$ (Raghavan, arXiv:2603.27045 v3, preprint). Established: exponent $1/9$ (Bloom–Sisask).
- *$[N]$, lower:* $N2^{-(C+o(1))\sqrt{\log_2N}}$, $C=2\sqrt{\log_2(24/7)}<2\sqrt2$ (2406.12290). Behrend's exponent $1/2$ in $\exp(-c\log^{1/2}N)$ is untouched; only $c$ improved.
- *$\mathbb F_p^n$:* $(cp)^n\le r_3(\mathbb F_p^n)\le(c_pp)^n$ with lower $c<\sqrt{7/24}\approx0.540$ and upper $\approx0.85$ for large $p$ (2406.12290).
- *$\mathbb F_3^n$:* $(2.2203\ldots)^n\le r_3(\mathbb F_3^n)\le(2.7552\ldots)^n$ (2606.12194). No improvement of the upper constant appears in 2606.12194 or in the 2024 survey 2408.02328.

**Where the methods stop.**
- Raghavan's method gives only $\exp(-\log^{1/5}N)N$ over $\mathbb F_q^n$, weaker than Ellenberg–Gijswijt; the polynomial method "has no known analogue" over $\{1,\dots,N\}$ (2603.27045, p. 2).
- Bohr-set density increments dilate the radius by $\alpha^{O(1)}$ per step over $O(\log(1/\alpha))$ steps, which naively costs two logarithms between $\mathbb F_q^n$ and $\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z$. Raghavan carefully tracks radii so the reciprocal exponents differ by $1+o(1)$ (1/5 versus 1/6), and says it is unclear whether $\exp(-\log^{1/2-o(1)}N)$ could be reached by Bohr-set increments; if so, one logarithm of loss would remain (2603.27045, p. 5).
- Bootstrapping step: the ideal input $\langle\mu_A\circ\mu_A,\mu_{A_1}\circ\mu_{A_1}\rangle^{1/2}\langle\cdots\mu_{A_2}\circ\mu_{A_2}\rangle^{1/2}\le C\langle\mu_A\circ\mu_A,\mu_{A_1}\circ\mu_{A_2}\rangle$ is not automatic; restoring it by repeated sifting makes the sets of density $\alpha^{O(\log(1/\alpha))}$ (2603.27045).
- Kelley–Meka methods over $\mathbb F_p^n$ remain far weaker than the exponential polynomial-method bound (2408.02328; Green's problem list, 2024 update).
- Lower bounds: classical constructions live in $[0,(p-1)/2]^n$ to avoid wrap-around and cannot exceed $((p+1)/2)^n$, so $c=1/2$ was a barrier (2406.12290, p. 3). A progression-free subset of the torus has measure zero, so only progressions with far-apart ends can be forbidden, and control must be on $x-z$, not $x-y$ (the latter statement is false). The area $7/24$ comes from an ad hoc polygon construction; no optimality is claimed.
- Direct products can never be asymptotically optimal; the Hametner–Tyrrell improvement is quantitatively negligible: about the 452nd decimal place of $2.2203\ldots$, with iterates shrinking super-exponentially. Equations of genus above one are outside the method, since direct products do not preserve freeness. Even with $d$ reduced to 6 (for $x+z-2y$), the bound does not beat the $p>5$ bound of 2406.12290 or the $\mathbb F_5^n$ record (2606.12194, Remarks 1.5, 4.1, 4.2).
- Slice-rank/polynomial arguments need the diagonal-tensor structure; extending to $k\ge4$ progressions was posed in 2408.02328, and a 2026 preprint (arXiv:2609.27044, Karingula–Lovett) is reported to show the slice-rank method cannot give exponential savings for $k\ge4$.

**Known open questions.**
1. Can the exponent $1/6$ for $r_3(N)$ be increased towards $1/2$? Can Bohr-set increments reach $\exp(-\log^{1/2-o(1)}N)$ (2603.27045, pp. 1, 5)? Is there an integer analogue of the polynomial method (2603.27045, p. 2; 2408.02328, §5)? Can combinatorial or Fourier methods over $\mathbb F_p^n$ approach the exponential saving of the polynomial method (Green's problem list, 2024 update)?
2. Improve the area $7/24$ of the two-dimensional building block, which would improve $C$ and $7/24$ (2406.12290, §§1–2); the authors call improvement plausible. Close the gap between $c\approx0.540$ and $\approx0.85$ for large $p$ (2406.12290, p. 2).
3. Conjecture 4.4 of 2606.12194: for a non-degenerate genus-one translation-invariant system over $\mathbb F_q$, an $L$-free $A\subseteq\mathbb F_q^n$ of size $c^n$ gives an $L$-free $B\subseteq\mathbb F_q^m$, $m>n$, with $|B|>c^m$. The authors do not optimise the dimension $d$ in their Theorem 1.3 (Remark 4.2).
4. Closing $(2.2203)^n$ versus $(2.7552)^n$ for cap sets.
5. Directions posed in 2408.02328, §5: algebraic methods for $k\ge4$ progressions in $\mathbb F_p^n$ (see the partial negative result above); how large can $S\subseteq\mathbb F_3^n$ be if it avoids 3-progressions with common difference in $\{0,1\}^n$ (per Green's list, Bhangale–Khot–Minzer and Liu showed density at least $(\log\log\log n)^{-c}$ forces such a progression); a precise characterization of problems solvable by these methods; unification of Chevalley–Warning, Stepanov, the Nullstellensatz and the progression-free method.

*Background.* Szemerédi's theorem (1975) extends Roth's theorem to $k$-term progressions; for $k=4$ Green–Tao give $(\log N)^{-c}$ and for $k\ge5$ Leng–Sah–Sawhney (arXiv:2402.17995) give $\exp(-(\log\log N)^{c_k})$.

## Result: Structure of sets with small doubling

**Origin.** Freiman's theorem says a set $A\subset\mathbb Z$ with $|A+A|\le K|A|$ lies in a proper generalized progression of dimension $O_K(1)$ and size $O_K(|A|)$. The question is how efficiently one can pass from the statistic $|A+A|/|A|$ to this description. Ruzsa and Green extended the qualitative statement to torsion groups and to all abelian groups. Marton conjectured a polynomial version, now called the polynomial Freiman–Ruzsa (PFR) conjecture. Sources: 2512.11217 §1; 2604.25893 §1.

**Milestones.**
1. *Freiman, 1966 and 1973 (books).* Qualitative theorem in $\mathbb Z$. Small $K$: if $|A-A|<3|A|-3$ then $A$ lies in an arithmetic progression of size $|A-A|-|A|+1$ (the $3k-4$ theorem; cited as Lev–Smeliansky, Acta Arith. 1995, and Tao–Vu, 2006). Freiman conjectured the same type of conclusion for doubling $<10/3$ (Bolyai Soc. Math. Stud. 11, 1999). Jin (Adv. Math. 2007) proved it for large sets with doubling below $3+\varepsilon_0$, $\varepsilon_0$ very small. [2604.25893 §1]
2. *Bogolyubov 1939; Ruzsa 1994 (Acta Math. Hungar.).* Bogolyubov: for dense subsets of a finite group, $A+A-A-A$ contains a large structured set. Ruzsa extended this to arbitrary sets of small doubling, as a step towards Freiman's theorem. [2512.11217 §1.1]
3. *Ruzsa 1999 (Astérisque 258); Green–Ruzsa 2007 (J. London Math. Soc. 75).* Torsion groups; then all abelian groups, with covering by $\exp(f(K))$ translates of a convex coset progression of dimension $\le f(K)$ and size $\le\exp(f(K))|A|$ for $f(K)=O(K^4\log^{O(1)}2K)$. [2512.11217 §1]
4. *Sanders, Anal. PDE 5 (2012).* $f(K)=\log^{O(1)}(2K)$. Also: $A+A-A-A$ contains a convex coset progression of dimension $O(\log^6 2K)$ and size $\ge\exp(-O(\log^{6+o(1)}2K))|A|$. With Konyagin's bootstrapping (reported in Sanders, Bull. AMS 50 (2013)): $f(K)=O(\log^{3+o(1)}2K)$, best known until 2025. For bounded torsion, Sanders showed $2A-2A$ contains a subgroup of size $\ge\exp(-C_m\log^4 2K)|A|$; Konyagin gave $\exp(C_{m,\varepsilon}\log^{3+\varepsilon}2K)$ translates (without $H\subseteq 2A-2A$). [2512.11217; 2404.02244 §1]
5. *Equivalent forms and variants.* Replacing convex bodies by ellipsoids gives an equivalent form of PFR over $\mathbb Z$ (Manners, arXiv 1709.09738). Replacing them by axis-parallel boxes is false (Lovett–Regev, Discrete Anal. 2017). In Ruzsa's function formulation over $\mathbb F_2^n$ ($f(x)+f(y)-f(x+y)$ takes values in a set $S$ of size $K$), the error set of the linear approximation cannot in general be taken inside a bounded multiple of $S$ (Green–Tao example, Green's Problem 49). [2512.11217 §1; Green, *100 Open Problems*]
6. *Gowers–Green–Manners–Tao, Ann. of Math. 201 (2025), arXiv 2311.05762.* In $\mathbb F_2^n$, $|A+A|\le K|A|$ implies $A$ is covered by $2K^{12}$ cosets of a subgroup of size $\le|A|$. Idea: minimize a functional of entropic Ruzsa distances and decrease the distance by "fibring" and a characteristic-2 endgame. Liao (arXiv 2404.09639) improved the exponent to 11 and then 9. [2404.02244 §1]
7. *Gowers–Green–Manners–Tao, arXiv 2404.02244 (preprint).* Torsion $m$: $A$ is covered by $(2K)^{O(m^3)}$ cosets of a subgroup of size $\le|A|$, and $H\subseteq\ell A-\ell A$ with $\ell\ll(2+m\log K)^{O(m^3\log m)}$. Idea: a multidistance chain rule, and the identity $\sum iX_{i,j}+\sum jX_{i,j}+\sum(-i-j)X_{i,j}=0$, which needs multiplication by elements of $\mathbb Z/m$. Consequence for odd $p$: $\|f\|_{U^3(\mathbb F_p^n)}\ge\eta$ gives a quadratic phase correlation $\gg\eta^{O(p^3)}$. (A polynomial inverse theorem for higher Gowers norms is a separate, much harder program.)
8. *Weak PFR.* Covering by $\exp(O(\log 2K))$ translates of a convex coset progression of dimension $O(\log 2K)$, with no size restriction, holds in every abelian group, since Marton's conjecture over $\mathbb F_2^n$ implies it (Green–Manners–Tao). [2512.11217 §1]
9. *Bloom, Discrete Anal. 2025 (arXiv 2501.11580).* For $A\subset\mathbb F_p[t]$ with $|A+tA|\le K|A|$ (a hypothesis on $A+tA$, not $A+A$), $A$ is covered by $K^{O(1)}$ translates of a generalized progression of rank $O(\log K)$ and size $K^{O(1)}|A|$.
10. *Eberhard–Green–Manners, Ann. of Math. 180 (2014), Thm 6.4.* If $\sigma_-[A]\le4-\varepsilon$, there is a one-dimensional progression $P$ with $|P|\gg_\varepsilon|A|$ and $|A\cap P|/|P|\ge1/2+c\varepsilon$. Sharp up to $c$, since density $>1/2$ in a progression forces $\sigma_-<4$. [2604.25893 §1]
11. *Green's Problem 30.* Given $|A-A|\le K|A|$, find a large structured $A'\subseteq A$ that obviously has $|A'-A'|\le(K+\varepsilon)|A'|$. Open for general $K$; Eberhard–Green–Manners answer it for $K<4$ (with an ineffective constant).

**Current frontier (November 2026).**
- *Integers and general abelian groups.* Raghavan, arXiv 2512.11217 (preprint, v2 Feb 2026): $A$ is covered by $\exp(f(K))$ translates of a convex coset progression of dimension $\le f(K)$ and size $\le\exp(f(K))|A|$, with $f(K)=O(\log(3K)\log\log(3K))$. Idea: minimize $d[X;Y]+\frac{\eta}{\log\log 3K}(d[X;U_A]+d[Y;U_A])$, $\eta=1/438$; minimizers have polynomial entropic growth of order $d=O(\log3K)$; then a Sanders-type Fourier/Bohr-set argument turns this into a convex coset progression of dimension $O(d\log d)$. The conjectured bound is $f(K)=O(\log2K)$. A union of $\exp(f)$ translates of such a progression has doubling $\exp(O(f))$, so $\log K$ is the natural limit. The bound beats the bounded-torsion bound when $m^3\gg\log\log K$.
- *Bounded torsion.* $(2K)^{O(m^3)}$ (arXiv 2404.02244, v2 2024); $\mathbb F_2^n$: $2K^9$ (arXiv 2404.09639, preprint).
- *Small doubling in $\mathbb Z$.* Jing–Mudgal, arXiv 2604.25893 (preprint, April 2026): for $\delta$ small enough in terms of $\varepsilon'$, if $|A+A|\le(4+\delta)|A|$ then either (1) a one-dimensional progression $P$, $|P|\gg_\delta|A|$, has $|A\cap P|/|P|\ge1/2-\varepsilon'$, or (2) a proper two-dimensional progression $Q$, $|Q|\gg_\delta|A|$, has $|A\cap Q|/|Q|\ge1-\varepsilon'$. Same for $A-A$ and for finite sets of reals. These are exactly the two extremal shapes (half-dense progression, full 2-D block), so the result is optimal apart from the dependence of $\delta$ on $\varepsilon'$. Corollaries: an "almost all sums" version, and $41A-40A$ contains a progression of dimension $\le2$ and size $\gg_\delta|A|$. Ingredients: arithmetic regularity, Kneser-type inequalities on tori, planar Brunn–Minkowski, geometry of numbers. The set $A_1=\{0,10N,2^N\}+[N]$ has $|A_1+A_1|=4|A_1|-6$ and shows that, unlike the continuous Figalli–Jerison setting, one cannot capture all of $A$ at these densities.

**Where the methods stop.**
- *Integers, endgame.* The bounded-torsion endgame fails in $\mathbb Z^d$: discrete Gaussians have doubling $\Theta(d)$ and are stable under sums and fibres, so the induction base case need not have $d[X_1;X_2]=O(1)$. [2512.11217 §5]
- *Integers, stability.* One needs a stable description of near-extremizers: near-minimal doubling should force near-Gaussian. Wasserstein-type stability of the Shannon–Stam inequality fails (Courtade–Fathi–Pananjady: heavy-tailed Gaussian mixtures, which nevertheless stay Ruzsa-close to Gaussians), and discrete stability is known only for log-concave laws. [2512.11217 §5]
- *Loss.* The $\log\log$ factor comes from $d\log d$; the author believes new ideas are needed to beat Theorem 1.4. The method gives no Bogolyubov–Ruzsa inclusion in $A+\dots+A$; Sanders' dimension $O(\log^6 2K)$ is not improved. [2512.11217 §§1.1, 5]
- *Torsion.* The identity $Z_1+Z_2+Z_3=0$ needs coefficients in $\mathbb Z/m$. The exponent $O(m^3)$ comes from step size $c/m^3$ in the decrement. The polynomial Bogolyubov statement is not obtained even for $m=2$; only $H\subseteq\ell A-\ell A$ with $\ell$ polylogarithmic. [2404.02244 §1]
- *Small doubling.* The proof only beats the Brunn–Minkowski–Kneser lower bound by $\delta$, so every loss must be $o(\delta)$. Constants from arithmetic regularity are ineffective. Discrete analogues of inverse Brunn–Minkowski often fail (there are $A\subset\mathbb Z^d$, genuinely $d$-dimensional, with $\sigma_-\le2d-2+1/(d-1)$). General-$K$ Question 1.1 does not appear to follow from PFR over $\mathbb Z$. The density thresholds $1/2$ in the lemmas are sharp. [2604.25893 §§1, 2, 5]

**Known open questions.**
1. PFR over $\mathbb Z$ and general abelian groups: is $f(K)=O(\log2K)$? [2512.11217 Conj. 1.5; 2604.25893 §1 cites Sanders 2013, Conj. 1.5]
2. Polynomial Bogolyubov: does $2A-2A$ contain a subgroup of size $K^{-O_m(1)}|A|$? In $\mathbb F_2^n$ (Green's Problem 50): does $10A$ contain a coset of codimension $O(\log1/\alpha)$? Best: codimension $O(\log^{4+o(1)}1/\alpha)$ (Sanders), $O(\log^{3+\eta})$ for $mA-mA$ (Kościuszko, building on Konyagin). In $[N]$: does $2A-2A$ contain a bounded-width Bohr set of dimension $O(\log1/\alpha)$? [2404.02244 §1; Green]
3. Efficient Bogolyubov–Ruzsa inclusion in $A+\dots+A$ in $\mathbb Z$. [2512.11217 §1.1]
4. Does a $\mathbb Z^d$-valued variable not concentrated on a hyperplane, with sufficiently high entropy, have entropic doubling $\ge\frac d2\log2-\varepsilon$ (the author thinks this may be within reach)? Is near-minimal doubling close to Gaussian in Ruzsa distance? Can Kac–Bernstein stability be discretized? [2512.11217 §5]
5. Can the $m$-dependence $(2K)^{O(m^3)}$ be reduced? [2404.02244 §1]
6. Green's Problem 30 for general $K$, with effective bounds; beyond Jin's $3+\varepsilon$, the Freiman conjecture for doubling $<10/3$. [2604.25893 §1; Green]
7. $U^3$ inverse theorem for odd-order groups with the bound shape $\log(1/\eta)\log\log(1/\eta)$, and an extension of Prendiville's relative inverse theorem to general finite abelian groups. [2512.11217 §1.1]

## Result: Restricted sumsets, from Erdős–Heilbronn to general forbidden pairs, and sums of algebraic dilates

**Origin.** Cauchy–Davenport bounds $|A+B|$ in $\mathbb F_p$ from below. Erdős and Heilbronn (Acta Arith. 9, 1964; see also Erdős–Graham 1980, p. 95) conjectured that deleting the diagonal sums costs only a constant: $|A\dot+A|\ge\min\{p,2|A|-3\}$. The general question is how small $|A+_RB|$ can be as a function of the structure of the forbidden set $R$.

**Milestones.**
- *Pollard (1974, J. London Math. Soc. 8).* If $N_i$ is the number of residues with at least $i$ representations $a+b$, then $N_1+\dots+N_t\ge t\min\{p,|A|+|B|-t\}$ for $t\le\min(|A|,|B|)$.
- *Dias da Silva–Hamidoune (1994, Bull. London Math. Soc. 26, 140–146).* Exterior-algebra proof of Erdős–Heilbronn; in two-set form $|A\dot+B|\ge\min\{|A|+|B|-3,p\}$.
- *Alon–Nathanson–Ruzsa (1995, Amer. Math. Monthly 102; 1996, J. Number Theory 56).* Polynomial method (Combinatorial Nullstellensatz, Alon, Combin. Probab. Comput. 8, 1999): new proofs, two different sets, and any restriction $P(a,b)\ne0$ with $P$ of low degree. Followed by Pan–Sun (J. Combin. Theory Ser. A 100, 2002) on $\{a+b: P(a,b)\neq0\}$ and Károlyi (JCTA 116, 2009) on the exceptional case.
- *Lev (2000, Electron. J. Combin. 7, R4).* Arbitrary $R$ with $m=|A|\le n=|B|$, $r=|R|$, in $\mathbb F_p$ (Pollard plus an optimisation in $t$; the core is due to Alon): $|A+_RB|\ge m+n-2\sqrt r-1$ if $m+n\le p+\sqrt r$ and $\sqrt r\le m$; $\ge p-r/(m+n-p)$ if $m+n\ge p+\sqrt r$; $\ge n-r/m$ if $\sqrt r\ge m$; interval examples show sharpness. In any finite group of order $q$: $m+n\ge q+1$ gives $|A+_RB|\ge q-r/(m+n-q)$, and for an injective map $\tau$ it gives $|A+^\tau B|>q-\sqrt q-\tfrac12$. In abelian groups, if $R$ has degree $\le1$ on both sides, at most $L$ pairs of $R$ have a given sum, and $A+_RB\ne A+B$, then $|A+_RB|>(1-\delta)(m+n)-(L+2)$ with $\delta=mn/(m+n)^2\le\frac14$.
- *Lev's two forms of the matching question.* Bilu–Lev–Ruzsa (Discrete Comput. Geom. 19, 1998) conjectured the Erdős–Heilbronn bound for an arbitrary map $\tau:A\to B$ ($|A|\le|B|$). Lev (2000) himself called this too optimistic and disproved it: for $k,d\ge1$ with $(2k+1)(2d-1)\le2p+1$ he built $\tau$ with $|A|+|B|=p+k+1-d$ and $|A+^\tau B|=|A|+|B|-k-1$. For *injective* $\tau$ he only *asked* (p. 3, "Is it true that…", adding that it "may not be the case") whether
 $|A+^\tau B|\ge |A|+|B|-3$ if $|A|+|B|\le p$; $\ge p-3$ if $|A|+|B|=p+1$; $\ge p-2$ if $|A|+|B|\ge p+2$. (Q)
 His examples: $|A|+|B|=\lfloor4p/3\rfloor$ with sumset $p-2$; $A=B$, $|A|=\frac{p+1}2$, sumset $p-3$.
- *Lev (2001, Period. Math. Hungar. 42).* Integer sumsets under "$(K,s)$-regular" restrictions: degree $\le s$ on both sides, at most $K$ pairs of $R$ with a given sum.
- *Bollobás–Leader–Tiba (Israel J. Math. 2025).* In $\mathbb F_p$, $\varepsilon>0$, $d=o(n)$, $n_0\le|A|=n\le(1-\varepsilon)p/2$, $R\subseteq A\times A$ of degree $\le d$: some $A'\subseteq A$ with $|A'|\le c$ has $|A+_RA'|\ge2|A|-1-2d$ (their Thm 35); for symmetric $R$, $|A+_RA|\ge2|A|-1-2d$. Also (Thm 5): if $|A|+|B|\le(1-\varepsilon)p$ and $|B|$ is small relative to $|A|$, three elements of $B$ already give $|A+\{b_1,b_2,b_3\}|\ge|A|+|B|-1$.
- *Ouyang (2025, arXiv 2503.09121 v3, preprint).* Using Bollobás–Leader–Tiba, Plünnecke–Ruzsa and Green–Ruzsa rectification (Bull. London Math. Soc. 38, 2006):
 (a) $A,B\subseteq\mathbb Z$, $|B|\le|A|$, degree $\le\Delta$ on $B$: $|A+_RB|\ge|A|+|B|-3\Delta$; degree $\le\Delta$ on both sides: $\ge|A|+|B|-1-2\Delta$, sharp.
 (b) $\mathbb F_p$, $|A|+|B|\le(1-\varepsilon)p$, $|B|\le c_\varepsilon p$, $p>4^{10\Delta}$ or $|A|\ge10\Delta$: the same two bounds. Consequently, for an arbitrary function $f:B\to A$, $|\{a+b: a\ne f(b)\}|\ge|A|+|B|-3$ (with $c_\varepsilon=(\varepsilon/10^{1600})^{10^{6400}/\varepsilon^4}$), and for matchings $R$ with $p>p_0(\varepsilon)$, $|A+_RB|\ge|A|+|B|-3$ — the first line of (Q) for $|A|+|B|\le(1-\varepsilon)p$. With degree $\le\Delta$ on both sides, $|A+_RB|\ge|A|+|B|-1-2\Delta$ for $|A|+|B|\le(1-\varepsilon)p$, $|A|\ge\beta\Delta$ or $p>\exp(O_\varepsilon(\Delta))$, no condition on $|B|$.
 (c) Constructions: in $\mathbb Z$ with $|A|=|B|=n\ge2\Delta$ and degree $\le\Delta$ on $B$, $|A+_RB|=|A|+|B|-1-\lfloor5\Delta/2\rfloor$. In $\mathbb F_p$, $A=B$ with $|A|=6\lfloor p/11\rfloor-3$ and a symmetric matching $R$ with $|A+_RA|\le p-3$ (missing sums $\{-2,-1,2\}$; from $\{0,1,2,3,5,6\}+11\mathbb Z$, in which each element is the unique point of $A$ on some translate of $\{0,1,4\}$); since $|A|+|A|\ge p+2$ for all primes $p\ge199$, this contradicts the third line of (Q) (extending a matching to an injective map only removes more sums). More generally, for every $\varepsilon>0$ there is $\delta>0$ with $|A|+|B|>(1+\delta)p-O(1)$, $|B|\le\varepsilon p$, a degree-1 relation and $|A+_RB|=p-3$. For functions: $|A|=p-(k-1)\ell-k+1$, $|B|=k\ell+2$, $|A+_RB|=p-k$ ($1\le\ell\le\lfloor\frac{p-k-1}{2k-1}\rfloor$); and $|A|=(1-2\varepsilon)p+O(1)$, $|B|=\varepsilon p+O(1)$, $|A+_RB|=|A|+|B|-4$. If $|A|+|B|\ge\lfloor\frac{2kp}{2k-1}\rfloor+1$, every function gives $|A+_RB|\ge p-k+1$, so the family is tight up to $k-2$ in $|A|+|B|$.
- *Conlon–Lim (2025, arXiv 2508.18586, preprint).* A different restriction-free direction: for algebraic $\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_k$ and every finite $A\subset\mathbb C$, $|A+\lambda_1\!\cdot\!A+\dots+\lambda_k\!\cdot\!A|\ge H(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_k)|A|-o(|A|)$, sharp up to the error (examples with error $O(|A|^{(d-1)/d})$). This settles the Krachun–Petrov conjecture $|A+\lambda A|\ge H(\lambda)|A|-o(|A|)$ (proved earlier for $\lambda=\sqrt2$, for $\lambda=(p/q)^{1/d}$ by Conlon–Lim, and for algebraic integers by Krachun–Petrov). Bukh (2008) had $(|p_1|+\dots+|p_k|+|q|)|A|-o(|A|)$ for $\lambda_i=p_i/q$. Proof: tight Brunn–Minkowski-type bound for sums of simultaneously diagonalisable maps in $\mathbb R^d$, a Freiman-type theorem for $\mathcal O_K$-progressions, and "lattice densities" along flags. For pre-commuting irreducible coprime integer matrices, $|L_0A+\dots+L_kA|\ge\prod_i(|a_{0i}|+\dots+|a_{ki}|)|A|-o(|A|)$, where $\det(\sum x_jL_j)=\prod_i\sum_ja_{ji}x_j$.

**Current frontier (November 2026).** Lev's question (Q): first line proved for matchings when $|A|+|B|\le(1-\varepsilon)p$, $p>p_0(\varepsilon)$ (Ouyang, preprint), open for $(1-\varepsilon)p<|A|+|B|\le p$ and for smaller $p$; second line ($=p+1$) neither proved nor refuted; third line false for all primes $p\ge199$ (Ouyang's matching on $A=B$), and a hypothesis $|A|+|B|\ge(1+\delta)p$ is necessary even for $|B|\le\varepsilon p$. For arbitrary functions $f:B\to A$: $\ge|A|+|B|-3$ holds for $|A|+|B|\le(1-\varepsilon)p$, $|B|\le c_\varepsilon p$, fails with $-4$ at $|B|\approx\varepsilon p$, $|A|\approx(1-2\varepsilon)p$. Integers with degree $\le\Delta$ on $B$: between $-1-\lfloor5\Delta/2\rfloor$ (construction) and $-3\Delta$ (theorem). Dilates: $H(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_k)|A|-O(|A|/\sqrt{\log_{(k)}|A|})$ (preprint), matched by a construction up to $O(|A|^{1-1/d})$.

**Where the methods stop.**
- Ouyang: the loss $3\Delta$ comes from the integer case after rectification; any improvement of Theorem (a) transfers verbatim to $\mathbb F_p$ (p. 4).
- The constants $c_\varepsilon$, $p_0$ are astronomically small (inherited from Bollobás–Leader–Tiba) and $c_\varepsilon$ is not linear in $\varepsilon$, so hypotheses like $|A|+c|B|<p$ are out of reach (p. 4); explicit $\beta,p_0$ in the two-sided theorem are not computed (p. 3).
- Beyond $|A|+|B|\ge p$ the method is blocked by the constructions in (c); those constructions gain only when $|A|+|B|-p>0$ (§4, p. 11).
- Lev (2000): arbitrary maps fail; improving the coefficient $1-\delta$ in his abelian-group theorem is called "the major open problem for generic restriction."
- Conlon–Lim: for non-integral $\lambda$ the local density of $A+\lambda A$ is only guaranteed to be $1/|\det L_1|$ times that of $A$, forcing asymmetric sums; no pair of flags works for all $l$ simultaneously; the continuous argument needs simultaneous diagonalisability, so pre-commuting is essential; the method and the one for $\lambda=(p/q)^{1/d}$ (error $O(|A|^{1-\sigma})$) are "quite orthogonal" (§7.1, §10).

**Known open questions.**
1. Ouyang: remove $\varepsilon$ from (b) for matchings (all $|A|+|B|\le p$); Conjecture: for any function $f:B\to A$ with $|A|+2|B|\le p$, $|A+_RB|\ge|A|+|B|-3$; Conjecture: $\mathbb Z$, degree $\le\Delta$ on $B$ gives $\ge|A|+|B|-1-\lfloor5\Delta/2\rfloor$.
2. Ouyang (Remark 4.2): if $\{0,1,4\}$ could be replaced by a set of size $\ge4$ keeping upper density $>\frac12$, the constructions would give $p-4$.
3. Lev (2000): the exact threshold for $|A|+|B|$ in the third line of (Q) and the truth of the second line; analogues in arbitrary finite groups and the best possible bounds; improve $1-\delta$ to $1$ or $1-\varepsilon$ when $r$ is small relative to $m+n$ ($\approx0.80$ for $B=A$).
4. Green, *100 open problems*, Problem 2: for $A\subset\mathbb Z$, $|A|=n$, is there $S\subseteq A$, $|S|\ge(\log n)^{100}$, with $S\dot+S$ disjoint from $A$? Known $|S|\ge(\log n)^{1+c}$ (Sanders, Canad. J. Math. 73, 2021; $c=1/69$ by Beker, arXiv 2501.10203) and $|S|\le e^{C\sqrt{\log n}}$ in general (Ruzsa, Ramanujan J. 9, 2005).
5. Green, Problem 25: for which $k$ does every partition $[N]=A_1\cup\dots\cup A_k$ have $|\bigcup_i(A_i\dot+A_i)|\ge N/10$? True for $k\ll\log\log N$, false for $k\gg N/\log N$ (Erdős–Sárközy–Sós, 1989); the range between is open.
6. Conlon–Lim (§10): error $O(|A|^{1-1/d})$ ("likely difficult"); any $O(|A|^{1-\sigma})$ with $\sigma=\sigma(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_k)$; the non-pre-commuting case, e.g. $L_0=\begin{psmallmatrix}0&1&0\\-1&0&0\\0&0&0\end{psmallmatrix}$, $L_1=\begin{psmallmatrix}0&0&1\\0&0&0\\-1&0&0\end{psmallmatrix}$, $L_2=\begin{psmallmatrix}0&0&0\\0&0&1\\0&-1&0\end{psmallmatrix}$ (irreducible, coprime): believed $|L_0A+L_1A+L_2A|\ge8|A|-o(|A|)$ for $A\subset\mathbb Z^3$ (box $[0,N)^3$ extremal), but not even $\ge C|A|$ is known; the corrected Bukh conjecture $|\sum L_iA|\ge(\sum|\det L_i|^{1/d})^d|A|-o(|A|)$ for general $k$.

## Format examples
The following three statements are already solved. They are included only to show the usual phrasing and level of precision of a research problem in this area (define every symbol, ask one or two sharp questions). They are not to be posed again.

Example 1. Let $p$ be a prime and $A\subseteq\mathbb F_p$ nonempty, and let $A\dot+A=\{a+a':a,a'\in A,\ a\ne a'\}$. Is it true that $|A\dot+A|\ge\min\{p,\,2|A|-3\}$?

Example 2. Let $A\subseteq\mathbb F_3^n$ contain no three distinct elements $x,y,z$ with $x+y+z=0$. Is there an absolute constant $c<3$ such that $|A|\le c^n$ for all $n$?

Example 3. Let $A\subseteq\mathbb F_2^n$ satisfy $|A+A|\le K|A|$. Is $A$ covered by at most $2K^{C}$ translates of a subgroup of size at most $|A|$, with $C$ an absolute constant?
